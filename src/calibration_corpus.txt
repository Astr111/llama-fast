# Calibration Corpus for TriAttention (arXiv:2604.04921)
# Natural text corpus covering mathematical reasoning, language modeling, scientific explanation, and algorithms.

Artificial intelligence and large language models have transformed modern computing by enabling machines to understand, generate, and reason with natural language at unprecedented scales. The core architecture powering these models is the Transformer, introduced by Vaswani et al. in 2017. Transformers rely on the self-attention mechanism, which computes pairwise compatibility scores between all tokens in a sequence to capture long-range contextual relationships.

In causal language modeling, tokens attend only to preceding tokens in the sequence. During inference, this autoregressive generation process presents distinct computational and memory challenges. While computing the initial prompt (prefill) is compute-bound and can be parallelized across tokens, the subsequent token-by-token generation (decode) phase is memory-bandwidth bound. To prevent redundant recomputation of key and value vectors for historical tokens, the key-value (KV) cache retains intermediate activations across all attention layers.

However, as the context window scales to tens or hundreds of thousands of tokens, the memory footprint of the KV cache grows linearly with sequence length. For a model with dozens of layers and attention heads, storing full-precision (FP16 or BF16) key-value states can quickly exceed the high-bandwidth memory (VRAM) of contemporary GPUs. This bottleneck severely restricts batch size, increases latency, and limits deployment on consumer-grade hardware.

To mitigate this constraint, two complementary paradigms have emerged: KV cache quantization and KV cache pruning. Quantization reduces the number of bits allocated to each cached vector. Recent advances such as TurboQuant demonstrate that polar Walsh-Hadamard transforms (WHT) can equalize outlier dimensions, enabling 2-bit, 3-bit, and 4-bit representations with minimal degradation in perplexity. Pruning, conversely, selectively evicts historical tokens deemed unimportant for future generation steps, effectively bounding the sequence length within a fixed budget.

TriAttention introduces a principled, training-free approach to KV cache eviction by operating in the pre-RoPE frequency domain. Traditional eviction strategies often rely on post-RoPE attention scores, which suffer from positional instability caused by rotary position embeddings. In contrast, researchers observed that prior to the application of RoPE, query and key vectors exhibit strong concentration around stable statistical centers. By projecting keys against the expected future query distribution using a trigonometric expansion, TriAttention estimates the future utility of cached tokens across multiple geometric horizons.

Consider a multi-head attention layer where query vectors Q and key vectors K are represented across frequency channels. When a new token is generated at time step t, its attention to a historical token at position s depends on both the semantic alignment of their representations and their relative positional distance delta = t - s. By calibrating the empirical mean and magnitude of query vectors across a representative text distribution, the model precomputes frequency-domain centers. During decoding, when the number of stored tokens exceeds the configured memory budget, TriAttention scores all candidate cells in parallel on the GPU and evicts the least significant entries, maintaining a compact and expressive context representation.

In addition to linguistic and scientific prose, mathematical reasoning requires precise tracking of intermediate algorithmic steps. For instance, when solving combinatorial optimization problems or analyzing matrix factorizations, the model must preserve structural anchors such as definitions, constraints, and transitional proofs. The dual-signal design of TriAttention ensures that attention heads with weak angular concentration fall back to magnitude-based norms, preventing accidental eviction of rare but critical reasoning tokens. Through the seamless synergy of low-bit TurboQuant representation and geometric TriAttention eviction, large language models achieve sustained throughput and bounded memory usage across extended reasoning tasks.
